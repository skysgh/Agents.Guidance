# Organisational Site Systems

An organisational site is a service surface that helps a defined audience find, understand and reach a capability. It may contain only a few pages, links, images and other media. Its lack of business logic does not make it disposable: people depend on its information, navigation, accessibility, availability and change history.

The project delivers more than page files. It delivers a coherent purpose, content, media, links, publication arrangement, acceptance evidence and handoff into continued maintenance. A corporate or intranet team may publish and maintain the result in an existing content-management platform, but that team is a different responsible boundary rather than an omission from the project design.

The site may be implemented as static content, a managed content site, a no-code application or another platform-composed solution. That implementation choice does not decide whether it is a service. The service question is whether the surface provides a meaningful capability with a purpose, audience, responsible boundary, content lifecycle and failure or recovery conditions.

## Intranet site

An intranet site serves people within an organisation or another established internal audience. It may explain an internal service, provide guidance, link to an application, support a workflow or connect people to internal records and capabilities.

An intranet site is a distinct deliverable even when it uses the same corporate content platform as other internal sites. Record its audience, purpose, content and media responsibility, authentication or access assumptions, links to the reached service, accessibility expectations, publication process, review timing, support route and retirement conditions.

## Extranet site

An extranet site serves an audience outside the organisation's internal population but within an intentional relationship, such as partners, suppliers, members, customers or represented organisations. It may provide information, launch a service client, support a partner journey or hand a person into an authenticated service.

An extranet site is a distinct deliverable because its audience, disclosure expectations, access handoff, content review and failure consequences may differ from an intranet site. Record which information is deliberately exposed, how authentication or referral works, who maintains the content and media, how a broken or stale link is found, and how the site is retired when the relationship or service changes.

## Shared delivery responsibilities

Intranet and extranet sites use similar delivery practices, but each instance still needs its own decision record. The project should agree:

- the audience, purpose and outcome the site supports;
- the pages, links, text, images, video and other media being delivered;
- the service or services the site describes or reaches;
- the team responsible for publication and continuing content change;
- the change-control path between the project and that team;
- accessibility, legal, brand, security and information-quality evidence;
- link, content, media, availability and recovery checks; and
- the review, update, handoff and retirement conditions.

The site team may own publication while the linked service team owns the capability and its authoritative decisions. The site must not create a second version of business meaning merely because it introduces the service. Its content should make the service understandable and reachable, while the reached service remains responsible for its own contracts, authorisation, durable state and consequential outcomes.

## System boundary and implementation

A site can be a distinct deliverable system when it has its own execution environment, publication boundary, dependencies, identity, lifecycle and failure behaviour. A project may instead deliver content and configuration into an existing corporate or intranet site system. In that case, the existing platform remains the execution system, but the project still records the site surface as a deliverable with an accountable maintenance handoff.

The site is not the same thing as the client system or service system it reaches. A site may link to a client application, embed or launch a no-code app, call several services or provide only curated information. The client or application remains responsible for its own interactions and contracts; the service remains responsible for authoritative capabilities and decisions.
