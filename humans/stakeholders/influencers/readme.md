# Service Influencers

Influencers are people, groups, organisations and obligations that shape a service without necessarily using or delivering it. They may impose duties, provide dependencies, represent interests, control resources or determine whether the service can be trusted and sustained.

Influence is not the same as hierarchy. An influencer may contribute evidence, constraints, challenge, funding, specialist knowledge or a dependency contract. The service should make that contribution visible without assuming that every influencer makes the decision or owns the resulting capability.

## Examples

Influencers may include regulators, auditors, legal, privacy and risk functions, procurement and finance, suppliers, hosting and identity providers, partner organisations, corporate communications and intranet teams, records-management functions, funders, public authorities, unions, advocacy groups and representative bodies.

A supplier may also deliver part of the service. A sponsor may also be a deliverer. An affected community may influence a decision through representation without operating the service. These relationships should be recorded by the responsibility being exercised rather than forced into one permanent category.
