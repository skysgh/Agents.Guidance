# Human Guidance Lifecycle Trace: Request Submission and Processing Status

## Purpose

This trace is the Phase 5 audit output for one representative capability. It follows a request from recognition through retirement and checks whether the guidance exposes authority, inputs, outputs, contracts, evidence, operational consequences and a route back when new information changes the meaning.

The capability is based on [Example 14: Ambiguity in Status States Before Authority Emerges](../../../IT/examples/14-ambiguity-in-status-states-before-authority-emerges/after.md). The request has an applicant, an organisational intake boundary, a processing team, Support, connected delivery or notification behaviour and an operational service. The status words are not treated as interchangeable: `Submitted`, `Received` and `Accepted for processing` carry different authority and evidence.

## Trace summary

The current guidance provides a strong path from ambiguity through decision, contract, testing, Support and Operations. The cold-start recheck confirms direct navigation for the Product Owner stopping point and specialist controls. [Example 17](../../../IT/examples/17-request-submission-full-lifecycle/after.md) now demonstrates the consolidated lifecycle evidence pattern, including specialist conditions, migration and retirement. A real project must still create its own records; this trace does not claim that an illustrative example is production evidence.

## Handoff observations

### Strong handoffs

- Example 14 carries ambiguous language into authority, state definitions, receipt contract, retry, duplicate behaviour, notifications, Support wording, operational alerting and recovery.
- Product guidance distinguishes strategic objectives from near-term outcomes and makes foundations part of value.
- Lifecycle guidance keeps Operations, Maintenance, Support, Testing and Service Provider responsibilities distinct.
- The conceptual, logical and physical distinction protects status meaning from becoming a screen label, database row or provider payload by accident.

### Open handoffs

- Product Owner to Architect sequencing now has a shared explanation in [Shared Requirements](../../../IT/shared/requirements.md): the Product Owner manages tickets against the architect's strategy, prioritises the System LDM before the next dependent LDM or interface, and listens to the logical blocking from foundation to furniture. Architecture owns the design and stopping point; the Product Owner protects the order against tactical business pressure and skipped rigour. Cold-start and lifecycle guidance are verified through the trace and Example 17.
- Shared specialist-control treatment now connects security, privacy, records, monitoring, change control and assurance responsibilities to their contributions, authority, evidence and return paths. Direct entry links from the natural Architect, Product Owner, Developer and Technical Lead routes and the full lifecycle example now pass the audit; real service evidence remains responsibility-specific.
- Cross-system test data, receipt reconciliation and retention or deletion evidence now have canonical treatment in [Deliverable Systems](../../../IT/reference/catalogues/deliverable-systems.md) and are demonstrated in Example 17.
- The current guidance now explains retirement responsibilities and demonstrates their application in Example 17 without claiming that the example replaces a real retirement record.

## Next implementation implications

The trace supports the following order of work:

1. Carry the Product Owner to Architect handoff into the conceptual, logical and physical model audit.
2. Carry the shared specialist control-responsibility treatment into the serious-system controls review.
3. Trace one concrete cross-system test and recovery scenario, including classified test data and reconciliation evidence.
4. Use the example index work to expose this lifecycle trace and the relevant examples by reader question.
5. Recheck the capability after those verification steps rather than adding route pages first.

## Audit limit

This trace tests the structure of the guidance against one representative capability. It does not prove that every real service will use the same status vocabulary, authority model, migration path or retirement regime. Those decisions remain local to the relevant domain, obligations, evidence and service context.
